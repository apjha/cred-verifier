      How the numbers are worked out

      The attacker gets a budget of G guesses during one rotation period. A random password with H bits of entropy falls with probability G/2H, so the page requires H ≥ log₂(G/p). G is worked out for online guessing (the attempt rate after lockout and rate limiting, over the rotation period, or 7 days if failed logins are monitored) and for offline cracking of a stolen hash (the cracking speed for that hash type over the rotation period). The larger requirement wins. p comes from the privilege level, divided by how likely the system is to be attacked at all. MFA relaxes the online target by 100× but not the offline one. The rotation period comes from an additive risk score.

      The entropy of generated passwords is counted exactly: minimum-count and first-character rules shrink the set of possible passwords, so the page doesn't use length × log₂(alphabet). Passwords come from crypto.getRandomValues with unbiased sampling. All constants match credverifier/exposure.py in this repository. They are deliberately round, conservative assumptions.
